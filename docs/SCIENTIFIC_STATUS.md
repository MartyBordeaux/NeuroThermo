# Scientific status after v3.9

Primary multi-sweep cohort: 18 cells, comprising 12 WT and 6 SCA3. Two additional accepted single-sweep cells are retained only as secondary descriptive records.

## Stable findings

Cell-level medians in the recorded cohort are `b=3.358` in SCA3 versus `3.004` in WT and `s=1.323` versus `0.546`, respectively. The recovered-animal medians show the same direction for both recovered SCA3 animals relative to both recovered WT animals. `s` is therefore the strongest stable group-associated HR coordinate, with `b` a more moderate one.

The final `r` medians are essentially overlapping (`0.0193` SCA3 versus `0.0209` WT), and `r` remains the least identifiable parameter. Earlier apparent genotype separation in `r` did not survive wide-bound stress and is not treated as a biological result.

Raw `kappa_I` is markedly lower in SCA3, but it strongly tracks capacitance. After capacitance normalization, recovered-animal medians overlap. Raw `kappa_I` is therefore treated primarily as an input-scaling/nuisance coordinate rather than an independent SCA3 phenotype.

Capacitance-normalized rheobase is markedly higher in the recorded SCA3 cells: cell medians are approximately `3.12 pA/pF` in SCA3 and `0.70 pA/pF` in WT.

## Fit quality

The four-parameter HR representation fits SCA3 less closely at the cell level than WT. Median cell loss is approximately 0.729 versus 0.520; median timing F1 is approximately 0.885 versus 0.982; median relative count error is approximately 0.183 versus 0.027. These fit-quality differences must accompany parameter comparisons.

## Statistical scope

Only two recovered animals are currently available per group. Formal animal-level genotype p-values are therefore not reported. Cell-level effect sizes describe the recorded-cell ensemble and must not be presented as independent-animal population inference.
